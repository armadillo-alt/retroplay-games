Keys and what the numbers mean

Keyboard or TV remote: arrows move, Enter is Start, Shift is Select, Z is B, X is A. SNES also has A for Y, S for X, and Q and W for the shoulder buttons.

NES game files end in .nes (or .unf); SNES files in .sfc or .smc. The page picks the right emulator by the file. Multi-game cartridges (such as a 42-in-1) work when the copy was made with the cartridge's own board type.

The speed test runs the emulator flat out for 10 seconds without drawing, then reports how many times faster than a real console this device is. 100% is just enough; 150% or more leaves room for the picture, the sound and busier games.

An NES needs far less than an SNES: a device that manages 100% for SNES manages about 350% for NES.

The SNES test cartridge was written for Retroplay: it switches on every picture layer and keeps the processor busy, which is about as heavy as an ordinary game. Chips inside some cartridges (Super FX, SA-1) make those games heavier.
